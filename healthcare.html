From someone who's lived it

I've spent more than 30 years in healthcare, in diagnostic radiology, CT and interventional cardiovascular work. I watched film turn into digital images, paper charts turn into electronic records, and now I'm watching AI arrive in the reading room and the exam room.

Every one of those changes was supposed to replace people. None of them did. What they changed was which skills mattered. The people who came out ahead learned the new system early, became the person everyone else asked for help, and never lost sight of the patient. As a clinical applications super user, that's the role I took on, and it shaped the rest of my career.

That's what this page is for: protecting the career you've built, seeing where you can move up, and helping lead the change instead of having it happen to you.

Alaina Tillage, CIIP, RT(R)(CT)(CI)

What's happening now

4 ways AI is reshaping healthcare work

01

Care jobs keep growing

An aging population keeps demand rising. Nurse practitioners are projected to be the fastest-growing healthcare occupation, up about 40% from 2024 to 2034, with medical and health services managers, physical therapist assistants and physician assistants close behind.

Source: BLS via Becker's Hospital Review

What you can do
  • Look at the growing roles one step up from yours, and what training they take.
  • Ask your employer about tuition help or career ladders before paying on your own.
  • Roles that combine hands-on care with judgment are growing fastest.
02

AI is taking over the paperwork

81% of physicians now use AI at work, up from 38% in 2023, mostly for documentation and research summaries. Some say AI scribes save 15 to 60 minutes a day, but in one 2026 poll 42% of users reported no time saved, so results depend on the tool and how it's set up.

Source: AMA 2026 survey via Health Data Management; AMA; Medical Economics

What you can do
  • Learn the AI documentation tool your organization uses, and become the person others ask.
  • Always review AI-drafted notes before signing. You are still responsible for what's in the chart.
  • Speak up during rollouts. Physicians and staff want a say in how these tools are chosen.
03

Imaging is the front line

Of the 1,524 AI-enabled devices the FDA had authorized through March 2026, 1,163 were for radiology, about 76% of the total. Imaging is where clinical AI is most established, so imaging staff see the change first.

Source: Radiology Business, June 2026

What you can do
  • Learn how AI triage and detection tools fit into your department's workflow.
  • Know the limits: AI flags findings, but people still make the call and talk to patients.
  • Skills that connect clinical work and IT, like PACS and imaging informatics, are in demand.
04

Admin and transcription jobs are shrinking

Medical transcriptionist jobs are projected to decline 5% from 2024 to 2034 as speech recognition takes over first drafts. In coding, billing and claims, AI is speeding up the work, but staffing firms say it raises the need for people who check and audit it.

Source: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook; Addison Group 2026 guide

What you can do
  • If you work in transcription, coding or billing, move toward auditing, compliance or editing AI output.
  • Add a credential in a growing area, such as health information or revenue cycle management.
  • Consider bridging into a patient-facing role if you enjoy working with people.
What's coming next

From AI that drafts to AI that runs whole processes

Today, most healthcare AI drafts notes or flags images for a person to review. The next step is AI that handles whole processes, like prior authorizations. In January 2026, Medicare began testing AI-assisted prior authorization review for certain services in six states: Arizona, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas and Washington. CMS says licensed clinicians make any recommendation to deny payment, and some members of Congress have criticized the program as a risk to timely care.

What to do now: strong documentation matters more than ever when software reads it first. Learn how AI decisions are made and appealed where you work, and be the person who can explain them to patients and coworkers.

How to climb without starting over

  • Ask about tuition help first. Many hospitals pay for the next credential, sometimes in exchange for a work commitment.
  • Look for "bridge" programs like LPN-to-RN or RN-to-BSN that count what you already know.
  • Become a super user. Volunteering to learn a new system first is one of the fastest ways to get noticed for informatics and leadership roles.

Lead the change

AI prompts for healthcare workers

Copy a prompt into any AI assistant and fill in the blanks. Never paste patient names, dates of birth, record numbers or any other patient information into a public AI tool, and follow your organization's AI policy.

Four prompts to start with

Study for a certification

Quiz me on [topic] for the [certification name] exam. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, then explain why it's right or wrong. Get harder as I improve.

Explain a new policy simply

Explain this policy or procedure in simple, everyday words, then list the three things that change for me day to day: [paste the policy, with no patient information].

Write a patient handout

Write a one-page handout explaining [procedure or condition] at a 6th-grade reading level. Use short sentences and a 'What to expect' section. I will check it for accuracy before using it.

Make a tip sheet for your team

I'm the go-to person for [new system or feature]. Write a one-page tip sheet for coworkers: the five things they'll use most, common mistakes, and who to ask for help.
