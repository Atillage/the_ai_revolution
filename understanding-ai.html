Your AI questions, answered

The questions people ask most

Will AI take my job?

AI is taking over tasks faster than whole jobs. The World Economic Forum expects 170 million new jobs and 92 million lost by 2030, so the bigger risk is standing still. Check which of your tasks are changing and plan your next skill.

Check your job →
Is ChatGPT safe to use?

For everyday tasks like drafting emails or explaining a topic, it's generally safe if you're careful. Never paste private information such as passwords, account numbers or patient and customer details, and double-check facts, because AI can sound confident and still be wrong. Follow your employer's rules at work.

Try safe, copy-ready prompts →
What is an AI agent?

An AI agent is AI that carries out a chain of steps on its own, like comparing prices, filling in a form and sending a confirmation, instead of answering one question at a time. Agents are the next big shift at work and at home.

Read Part 4 of the beginner series →
Which AI assistant should I use?

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot all have free versions and handle everyday tasks well. Pick one, use it for a week on real tasks, and switch only if it doesn't fit.

Compare the big AI players →
Do I still need a degree to get a good job?

Less than before. Governors in more than 30 states have acted to remove degree requirements from state jobs, and federal hiring is shifting toward tests of real skills. Apprenticeships and certificates are real paths in.

See where hiring is opening up →
How is AI changing health care jobs?

AI is taking over paperwork like notes, coding and prior authorizations, and imaging is where clinical AI is most established. Hands-on care keeps growing, so the safest moves combine patient care or clinical skill with judgment.

See healthcare career ladders →
How do I spot an AI scam?

Scammers now use AI to clone voices, fake videos and write convincing messages. Be suspicious of any urgent request for money or codes, even from a familiar voice, and confirm by calling back on a number you already know.

AI words, explained

20 words everyone's using

Tap "Share this word" to copy a link you can text to a friend.

Artificial intelligence (AI)
Computer systems that do things that normally take human thinking, like understanding language, spotting patterns or making predictions.
Algorithm
A set of step-by-step rules a computer follows. Apps use them to decide what to show you, from search results to rent prices.
Machine learning
A way of building AI where the computer learns patterns from lots of examples instead of being given every rule.
AI model
The trained "brain" behind an AI tool. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each run on their own models.
Large language model (LLM)
An AI model trained on huge amounts of text so it can read, write and answer questions in everyday language.
Generative AI
AI that creates something new: text, images, music, video or code.
Chatbot
A program you talk to by typing or speaking. Today's AI chatbots can hold a real back-and-forth conversation.
AI assistant
A chatbot built to help with tasks, such as drafting, planning, summarizing or answering questions.
Prompt
What you type or say to an AI tool. Clear prompts with context get much better answers.
AI agent
AI that carries out a chain of steps on its own, not just one answer at a time.
Hallucination
When AI states something false with full confidence. It's why you always check important facts.
Training data
The examples an AI model learned from. Gaps or slants in the data show up in the answers.
Bias
When an AI system treats some people or groups unfairly, often because of its training data.
Multimodal
AI that works with more than one kind of input, like text, photos, voice and video together.
Computer vision
AI that understands images and video. It's how robot vacuums dodge socks and how scans get flagged.
Speech recognition
AI that turns spoken words into text, used in voice assistants and medical transcription.
Deepfake
A fake video, photo or voice made with AI to look or sound like a real person.
Automation
Using technology to do a task with little or no human effort. AI makes far more tasks automatable.
On-device AI
AI that runs on your phone or gadget instead of in the cloud, which can be faster and more private.
Humanoid robot
A robot shaped roughly like a person, with arms and legs, built to work in spaces designed for people.
