Pillar 3 · The Future of Finance

AI is rebuilding how money works.

What happens to my money when AI changes how we earn, pay and save?

AI agents are starting to shop and pay for people. Money is moving into apps. The government is opening investment accounts for babies. And paychecks may get less predictable. Here's what's changing, how to get ready, and how to start investing.

What's happening now

4 ways AI is reshaping your money

01

AI will spend money for you

Visa, Mastercard and PayPal have built systems that let AI assistants shop and pay on your behalf, inside tools like ChatGPT and Gemini. You set the limits and the AI completes the purchase. Visa predicts millions of people will use AI agents to buy things by the 2026 holiday season.

Source: Visa (Dec 2025)

What you can do
  • Before you let an AI buy anything, set a spending limit and require your approval for every purchase.
  • Turn on instant purchase alerts on every card you connect to an AI tool.
  • Use one card for AI shopping, ideally a credit card or a virtual card number, not your main debit card.
  • Check your statements weekly. If an AI makes a purchase you didn't approve, dispute it with your card company right away.
02

Your money is moving into apps

Elon Musk's X launched X Money for U.S. Premium subscribers in July 2026, with the goal of making X a place to hold, send and spend money. Payment apps, AI budgeting tools and app-based banks all want to hold your paycheck. Not all of them work like a bank, and not all of them protect your money the same way.

Source: Digital Trends (July 2026)

What you can do
  • Before you keep money in any app, find out if it's FDIC-insured, and up to how much.
  • Keep your emergency cushion in an insured bank or savings account, not in a payment app balance.
  • Turn on two-factor sign-in for every money app, and use a different password for each.
  • Read what the app does with your data before you connect your bank account.
03

Investing now starts at birth

Trump Accounts launched on July 4, 2026. Parents can open an investment account for any child, and children born during President Trump's second term get $1,000 from the government. The money is invested in U.S. stock index funds and locked until age 18, then can be used for things like school or a first home. Some employers and donors are adding money too.

Source: AP via KPRC (July 2026)

What you can do
  • Had a baby since 2025? Open an account at trumpaccounts.gov to claim the $1,000.
  • Older children can have an account too, even without the $1,000.
  • Ask your employer whether it adds money to employees' children's accounts.
  • Ask grandparents and relatives to give to the account instead of buying another toy.
04

Paychecks may get less predictable

Elon Musk says AI and robots could make work optional in 10 to 20 years, with a 'universal high income' paid by the government. Critics say no government could afford it, and nothing like it exists today. Meanwhile, 37% of adults couldn't cover a surprise $400 expense with cash.

Source: Fox Business (Musk); Federal Reserve (2025)

What you can do
  • Don't plan around a future check. Build a cushion of at least one month of bills, then three.
  • Add a second, smaller source of income that uses human skills: care, trades, teaching or local services.
  • If you earn side income, set aside money for taxes every time you're paid.
  • Use the cushion calculator below to see where you stand.

02

Let AI do your money chores

Copy a prompt into any free AI assistant, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and fill in the blanks.

Find forgotten subscriptions

Here are the charges from my last two months of bank and card statements (descriptions and amounts only): [paste here]. List every charge that repeats, how often it bills and what it costs per year. Flag any that look like ones I may have forgotten about.

Build a bill calendar

Here are my monthly bills with due dates and amounts: [list]. I get paid on [dates] and take home about $[amount] each time. Show which paycheck should cover which bills, and warn me about any weeks that will be tight.

Make a 30-day cushion plan

My must-pay bills are about $[amount] a month and I have $[amount] saved. Suggest 5 realistic ways to free up $25 to $50 a week without cutting groceries or medicine, then turn them into a simple 30-day plan with one small step each week.

Stay safe: never paste account numbers, passwords or your Social Security number into an AI tool.

03

Spot AI money scams

Scammers now use AI to copy voices, faces and bank messages. Know the warning signs before they call.

  • A loved one's voice asking for money fast. AI can copy a voice from a short clip. Hang up and call them back on a number you know.
  • A "can't-lose" AI trading bot or crypto deal, often in a video with a famous face. Guaranteed returns are a scam.
  • Pay by gift card, wire, crypto or payment app. Real banks, agencies and family emergencies don't demand these.
  • A text or email from "your bank" asking you to verify. Don't click. Open your bank's app yourself.
  • Set a family code word that a caller has to know before anyone sends money.

Learn to invest

How to start investing in stocks

AI may change who earns a paycheck, but owning a small piece of the companies doing the work is open to everyone. You don't need a lot of money. You need a plan and time.

  1. Build your cushion first

    Money you might need in the next few years belongs in savings, not stocks. Pay off high-interest credit cards too: it's a guaranteed return.

  2. Grab any free money

    If your job has a 401(k) or 403(b) with an employer match, put in at least enough to get the full match. That's free money.

  3. Pick the right account

    No match at work? A Roth IRA lets your investments grow tax-free for retirement. For goals that aren't retirement, use a regular brokerage account.

  4. Start with a low-cost index fund

    An index fund buys a little of hundreds of companies at once. An S&P 500 index fund owns about 500 of the largest U.S. companies, including many building AI. Look for a fee (expense ratio) of 0.10% or less.

  5. Put it on autopilot

    Set up an automatic weekly or monthly deposit, even $10 or $25. Many apps let you buy fractions of a share, so small amounts work.

  6. Stay in for the long run

    Stocks go up and down, sometimes a lot. Plan to leave the money for 5 years or more, and don't sell in a panic when the market drops.
