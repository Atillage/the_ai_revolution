What's happening now

4 ways AI is changing work

01

AI changes tasks before whole jobs

Microsoft researchers studied how people use its Copilot AI assistant at work and found it helps most with researching, writing and communicating. The lead researcher said the work “does not indicate it can fully perform any single occupation.” The World Economic Forum expects 170 million new jobs and 92 million lost by 2030.

Source: World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025; Microsoft Research (2025)

What you can do
  • List the five tasks that take up most of your week.
  • Use the job check above to see which ones AI is taking on.
  • Get good at using AI for those tasks before someone else does it for you.
02

Desk work is changing first; hands-on work lasts longer

The jobs that overlap most with AI involve knowledge work: writers, translators, sales and customer service. The least affected include nursing assistants, phlebotomists and hazardous materials workers. Elon Musk calls AI a “supersonic tsunami” for digital jobs, and an April 2025 executive order set a goal of one million new apprenticeships a year.

Source: Microsoft Research via Fortune (2025)

What you can do
  • If your job is mostly screen work, add a people skill or hands-on skill AI can't easily do.
  • Consider a paid apprenticeship in a trade, health care or IT (see Education).
  • If your job is hands-on, learn the digital tools around it: quotes, scheduling, records.
03

AI reads your application before a person does

87% of hiring managers say their company uses AI in at least one part of hiring, and 58% use it to screen resumes and applications.

Source: Resume Genius 2026 Hiring Trends Report (survey of 1,000 U.S. hiring managers)

What you can do
  • Use the exact words from the job post for skills you really have.
  • Keep your resume format simple: plain headings, no tables or graphics.
  • Use AI to tailor your resume, then rewrite it in your own voice. Never let it invent experience.
  • Ask someone inside the company to refer you. A person still beats the filter.
04

One paycheck may not be enough

35% of U.S. workers now have a side hustle or more than one job, according to a 2026 ZipRecruiter survey. Side hustlers report earning an average of $1,242 a month. AI makes it easier to start: it can write your listings, quotes and follow-ups.

Source: ZipRecruiter (2026); LendingTree (2026)

What you can do
  • Start small: 3 to 5 hours a week with a skill you already use.
  • See the four paths in “Build a second paycheck” below.
  • Check your employer's rules on outside work before you start.
What's coming next

From AI helpers to AI agents

So far, most people use AI to help with one task at a time: write this email, summarize that report. The next wave is AI "agents" that carry out whole chains of work on their own, like finding a supplier, comparing prices, placing the order and updating the spreadsheet. As that spreads, the most valuable workers will be the ones who can set up, check and steer those agents, and who handle the people, judgment and hands-on work they can't.

What to do now: get comfortable handing AI a multi-step task and checking its work. The prompts below are a good place to start.

Put AI to work for you

Let AI handle the busywork

Copy a prompt into any AI assistant, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and fill in the blanks. Follow your employer's rules on AI tools, and never paste private customer, patient or company information.

Four prompts for everyday work

Draft a hard email

Help me write a clear, polite email to [person] about [situation]. I want to [goal]. Keep it under 150 words and calm in tone.

Turn meeting notes into action items

Here are my notes from a meeting (no private customer or patient details): [paste]. List the decisions, the action items with who owns each one, and any open questions.

Write your weekly update in 5 minutes

Here's what I worked on this week: [list]. Turn it into a short update for my manager: wins, what's in progress, and anything I need help with.

Turn a messy process into a checklist

Here's how I do [task] at work: [describe]. Turn it into a clear numbered checklist a new coworker could follow.

Skills over degrees

Where hiring is opening up

More than 70 million U.S. workers, about half the workforce, built their skills through work, the military, community college or training instead of a four-year degree. Employers and governments are starting to hire for those skills.

01

Tear the Paper Ceiling employers

A national campaign led by Opportunity@Work and the Ad Council. Employers that join commit to looking past degree screens to hire skilled workers.

See the campaign ↗
02

Federal jobs

The Chance to Compete Act of 2024 and the 2025 Merit Hiring Plan move federal hiring away from degree requirements and toward tests of real job skills.

Search USAJOBS ↗
03

State jobs

Governors in more than 30 states have acted to remove degree requirements from state government jobs. Maryland was the first, in 2022.

How to look: search "[your state] state jobs" and look for "or equivalent experience."

04

Paid apprenticeships

A 2025 executive order set a goal of one million new apprenticeships a year. You earn a paycheck while you train in IT, health care, the trades and more.

Search Apprenticeship.gov ↗

Sources: Tear the Paper Ceiling; U.S. Office of Personnel Management; National Governors Association. Next step: build a Skills Passport to show employers what you can do.

Build a second paycheck

Four realistic ways to earn on the side

You don't need a big idea. Most side income starts with a skill you already use, a few hours a week, and AI to handle the writing and admin.

Easiest start

Sell a skill you use at work

Scheduling, bookkeeping, data entry, writing, customer follow-up. Offer it to small businesses or on freelance sites like Upwork or Fiverr.

AI helps: write your profile, proposals and quotes.

Hard for AI to replace

Local hands-on services

Handyman work, cleaning, pet care, moving help, yard work. Neighbors and apps like Nextdoor bring steady local customers.

AI helps: price jobs, answer messages and post ads.

Uses what you know

Teach or tutor

Tutor students, train new hires in your field, or turn what you know into a short guide or class you sell online.

AI helps: build lesson plans, worksheets and quizzes.

Steadier hours

Part-time remote work

Part-time remote roles in customer support, admin, scheduling and data. Use screened job boards to avoid scams.

AI helps: tailor your resume and practice interviews.

Before you start

  • Keep it separate. Open a separate bank account for side income so taxes and records stay simple (see banking and tax help).
  • Save for taxes. Side income usually has no taxes taken out. Set some aside from every payment; a tax tool or tax pro can tell you how much.
  • Watch for scams. Real jobs never ask you to pay to start, buy equipment up front, or deposit a check and send money back.
  • Check your job's rules on outside work and conflicts of interest.
