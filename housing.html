What's happening now

4 ways AI is already shaping your housing

01

It decides who gets approved

Many rental applications are scored by software before a person looks at them. In a landmark case, SafeRent settled a lawsuit claiming its scores unfairly marked down Black and Hispanic applicants and people using housing vouchers, and agreed to stop scoring voucher holders. Mortgage lenders are adding AI to underwriting and home valuations too, and 46% of homebuyers say lenders shouldn't run automated valuations without their approval.

Source: AP via Fortune (SafeRent, 2024); Cotality homebuyer survey (2026)

What you can do
  • Turned down for an apartment because of a screening report? The landlord has to tell you which company made it, and you can get a free copy from that company within 60 days.
  • Dispute anything wrong in the report directly with the screening company.
  • Ask whether a person can review your application, especially if you use a housing voucher.
  • Buying? You have the right to a copy of every appraisal and valuation your lender used.
02

It helps set your rent

Rent-pricing software from RealPage was at the center of a federal antitrust case over landlords allegedly using shared data to push rents up. RealPage settled with the Justice Department without admitting wrongdoing, and in October 2026 a court approved nearly $360 million in settlements involving 41 defendants. More than 22 states have introduced bills on algorithmic rent pricing.

Source: The Close (Oct 2026); Private Equity Stakeholder Project (Jan 2026)

What you can do
  • Ask the leasing office how rent is set, and compare your renewal offer with nearby listings before you sign.
  • If you rented from a large property company, check whether it's part of a rent-pricing settlement. Use only the official court-approved claims site, and never pay anyone to file for you.
  • Check whether your state or city has passed rules on rent-pricing software.
03

It changes where you can live

About 1 in 4 paid workdays in the U.S. now happen at home, up from roughly 7% before 2020. For many people, the job no longer decides the zip code, but moving only pays off if the math works.

Source: WFH Research (Stanford), 2026

What you can do
  • Before moving somewhere cheaper, add up the full picture: rent or mortgage, taxes, insurance, commute days, and travel back to the office.
  • Get your remote or hybrid arrangement in writing before you move.
  • Ask: if I had to go back to the office full time, would this still work?
04

It's changing how homes get built

Robotic 3D printers built Wolf Ranch in Texas, a 100-home neighborhood by ICON and Lennar that became Lennar's top-selling community in Central Texas. The homes start just under $400,000. It's a real step toward building faster, but it isn't low-cost housing yet.

Source: Builder magazine; CNBC via NBC Miami

What you can do
  • Treat new building tech as a long-term trend, not a reason to wait to buy or rent.
  • If you look at a 3D-printed or factory-built home, compare it like any other home: price per square foot, insurance, warranty and resale.
  • Follow local zoning news. Rules on smaller homes and rental units shape what gets built near you.
What's coming next

Your AI agent vs. their AI agent

Landlords and lenders already use AI to screen, price and approve. Next, renters and buyers will have AI agents of their own that search listings, compare the true monthly cost, flag unusual lease terms and draft offers or negotiation emails. Homes themselves are starting to be built faster by robots and factory methods. The people who come out ahead will be the ones who know their numbers and their rights, and use AI to check the other side's math.

What to do now: start using AI for the paperwork side of housing. The prompts below are a good place to start.

Your home as a safety net

Make your housing work when income is uncertain

If AI changes your job, your housing costs are usually the biggest bill you can't skip. These moves give you room to adjust.

01

Know your number

If housing takes more than 30% of your income, the government counts you as cost-burdened, and a job change would hit hard.

$
$
Rent or mortgage, plus utilities, insurance and any HOA fees.
No email required. Your numbers stay on your device.
02

Have a plan B before you need it

Decide now what you'd do if your income dropped, so you're not deciding in a panic.

  • List your options: a roommate, a cheaper unit, moving in with family, or renting out space.
  • Talk to your landlord or lender early. Payment plans are easier to get before you fall behind.
  • Get free expert help. A HUD-approved housing counselor can help with renting, buying, credit and avoiding foreclosure.
03

Make your home earn or save

Small changes can take real pressure off your budget.

  • Rent a spare room for a month or more, for example to traveling nurses (see partners below).
  • Check the rules first: your lease, HOA and city may limit rentals, and rental income is taxable.
  • Cut the bills you control. Ask your utility about a free or low-cost energy audit and budget billing.
Put AI on your side

AI prompts for renters and owners

Copy a prompt into any AI assistant, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and fill in the blanks. Remove names, account numbers and Social Security numbers before you paste any document.

Four prompts for housing paperwork

Dispute a tenant screening report

Help me write a short, polite letter to [screening company] disputing errors on my tenant screening report. The errors are: [list them]. Ask them to correct or remove these items and send me an updated copy. Keep it under 200 words.

Push back on a rent increase

My rent is going up from $[current] to $[new]. Similar units nearby list for [prices]. I've paid on time for [number] years. Write a polite email to my landlord asking for a smaller increase or a longer lease at today's rate.

Explain my lease in plain English

Explain this lease in plain English. List every fee, penalty and renewal term, and flag anything unusual I should ask about before signing: [paste the lease, with names and account numbers removed].

Check the math on a remote-work move

I work remotely and earn $[amount] a year. Compare living in [current city] and [new city]: rent, state and local taxes, insurance, utilities and [number] trips back to the office a year. Show monthly totals in a table and tell me what to double-check.

Before you send anything

  • Check every fact. AI can get laws and numbers wrong. Your state or city rules are what count.
  • Keep it polite and in writing. Email or letters create a record if you need it later.
  • Know when to get help. For an eviction notice or foreclosure, contact a housing counselor or local legal aid right away.
